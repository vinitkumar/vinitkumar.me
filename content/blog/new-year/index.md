The first week of 2026 is already here. This time, I do not want to begin with a grand resolution that disappears before January ends. I want a small set of durable intentions instead.

I want to write more, read much more, and recover the pure fun of programming. I want to produce a substantial body of code, learn one or two new languages, and use them to build something useful.

Over the past year, I have also learned to use AI more effectively. It can extend my reach and improve my output, but I do not want convenience to replace understanding. Alongside AI-assisted work, I plan to write a compiler and explore low-level C by hand. Those projects will force me to reason carefully and keep the fundamentals sharp.

My tools need less attention, not more. I am happy with my Neovim setup and no longer want to spend endless hours rearranging it. That time belongs in the work itself.

One tool I do want to understand better is Git. After using it since 2010, I only recently discovered how useful worktrees can be. They make concurrent work on one repository far cleaner, especially when several AI-assisted tasks run in parallel. The lesson was humbling: years of familiarity with a tool do not mean I have learned it deeply.

So that is the direction for 2026: fewer declarations, more deliberate practice; less configuration, more creation; and enough space left over to enjoy life.

I wish you a fulfilling year.
