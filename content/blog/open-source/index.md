I joined GitHub in 2010. It quickly became my main place for reading and writing code, and other people's projects gave me my first real education in open source development.

Since then, I have published projects in JavaScript, Python, and Go. Open source gives me room to follow an idea, learn in public, and build something beyond the immediate needs of my job.

That freedom has a cost: time and energy are limited. For the previous eighteen months, my paid work had consumed most of both. I tackled difficult scaling problems, tuned performance, and shipped features that mattered to users. I valued that work, but it left little space for projects of my own.

I have not solved the balance perfectly. What helps is giving open source a small, regular place in my week instead of waiting for a large block of free time to appear. A modest rhythm is enough to keep curiosity alive.

I am writing code in public again. I hope to keep going—not by sacrificing the rest of my life, but by making deliberate room for work that teaches me something new.
